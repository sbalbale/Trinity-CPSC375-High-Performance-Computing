# Informal Writing Samples

These are curated excerpts from Sean Balbale's reading response notes (RNTs), free-writes, and casual reflections. This is Sean's most unfiltered voice — opinionated, direct, and personal. Use these as anchors when generating blog posts, journal entries, Slack messages, or any writing where the register is conversational but thoughtful.

---

## Sample 1: Strong Disagreement (RNT 9/4/2025 — The Squirrel Story)

> I found the squirrel story to be very interesting. I've consistently grown up with the internet, and as an engineer, I believe I have a good understanding of how it works and what it is. It never even occurred to me that people just think it to be a magical, separate realm. To me, the internet is everywhere (with some particular exceptions). It is more of a graph/web of connections between devices. [...] I fundamentally disagree with the author. The internet isn't nowhere, but it isn't only somewhere. It's everywhere. [...] I just think it's a bit naive to say that it has one specific place, even within the realm of a data center.

### What to notice:
- **Opens with engagement** ("very interesting") before pivoting to disagreement.
- **Grounds opinion in identity:** "as an engineer, I believe..."
- **Doesn't hedge the disagreement:** "I fundamentally disagree." "I just think it's a bit naive."
- Uses a **tricolon reframe**: "The internet isn't nowhere, but it isn't only somewhere. It's everywhere."
- Parenthetical adds personality: "(with some particular exceptions)."

## Sample 2: Connecting Ideas Across Readings (RNT 9/11/2025)

> I really enjoyed the thorough line of the hidden material costs of technologies that we often imagine as "immaterial." Sacasas argues that digital culture encourages us to forget our embodied existence, while Taft points out that companies like OpenAI and Google obscure the actual energy costs of AI. In both cases, the virtual hides the physical.

### What to notice:
- **Synthesis across multiple sources** in a single paragraph.
- The final sentence — "In both cases, the virtual hides the physical" — is a **Sean-style punchline**: short, declarative, and reframes everything before it.
- Uses author names naturally, not as citations.

## Sample 3: Engineering Perspective on Non-Engineering Topics (RNT 9/11/2025)

> The suggestion of routing smaller models makes perfect sense from an engineering perspective. It's load balancing. If you use tools like Chat GPT or Google Gemini, you can see that they default to their smaller models. That said, companies resist publishing their energy usage because it makes them look bad and could give away too much about their infrastructure.

### What to notice:
- **Translates complex concepts into engineering analogies:** "It's load balancing."
- Two-word sentence for emphasis: "It's load balancing."
- "That said" as a pivot — concedes the technical solution before noting the business reality.

## Sample 4: Personal Reflection with Analytical Edge (RNT 10/16/2025 — Free Write)

> On TikTok, I am a dog-obsessed gym rat. Most of the videos that I interact with are workout and dog-related, with the exception of the videos that my girlfriend sends me. I think the algorithm keeps me focused on those topics. I don't learn or follow politics the way I do on YouTube. All I do is look at workout equipment and cute animals. I think this loop deemphasizes education and expanding my knowledge, as well as disconnects me from what's going on in the world. It keeps me focused on fitness, which is good, but not exactly mentally stimulating.

### What to notice:
- **Self-aware and self-deprecating:** "I am a dog-obsessed gym rat."
- Moves from **personal observation → systemic critique** within a single paragraph.
- "Which is good, but not exactly mentally stimulating" — classic Sean: acknowledges the nuance, doesn't moralize.

## Sample 5: Emotional Response to Reading (RNT 10/28/2025 — O'Neil CH 3)

> This chapter provides an eye-opening view of how something seemingly helpful, like the US News and World Report college rankings, could become what O'Neil calls a weapon of math destruction. It made me rethink how much we trust these rankings. The core idea is that this ranking system grew to such a massive scale that it created damaging feedback loops and perverse incentives throughout higher education.

### What to notice:
- Opens with **emotional reaction** ("eye-opening") then immediately grounds it in the argument.
- Uses the author's own terminology: "weapon of math destruction."
- Identifies the **mechanism**: "feedback loops and perverse incentives."
- Connects reading to personal experience: "It made me rethink how much we trust these rankings."

## Sample 6: Quick Takes / Free-Writes

> I think social media is a double-edged sword. It can be a useful tool for networking and to keep up to date with friends and family. That said, social media is becoming increasingly addictive nowadays. It is straying away from just being a tool to becoming something designed to increase profit and engagement.

> I think the best example I have is when I need to go to sleep, but all I want to do is just doom scroll on my phone. Watching Instagram Reels or TikTok, or YouTube Shorts is really addictive, and sometimes I'll ignore what I should be doing (going to bed) for the enjoyment of watching videos.

### What to notice:
- **Conversational but structured.** Even in a free-write, there's a clear claim → evidence → implication flow.
- Uses "That said" as a transition (recurring pattern).
- Personal anecdotes used as evidence: "I need to go to sleep, but all I want to do is just doom scroll."
- Parenthetical for casual aside: "(going to bed)."

## Key Informal Writing Patterns

### Openers:
- "I found X to be very interesting/thought-provoking/powerful."
- "I really enjoyed..."
- "This chapter/article was [adjective]."
- Then IMMEDIATELY pivot to *why*.

### Transitions:
- "That said," (concession pivot — extremely frequent)
- "I also..." / "I also think..."
- "The most interesting/concerning part was..."

### Voice:
- First person, always.
- Direct opinions: "I fundamentally disagree." "I just think it's a bit naive."
- Self-aware: willing to admit what he doesn't know or where he's biased.
- Engineering lens applied to everything: algorithms, load balancing, feedback loops, systems thinking.

### Structure:
- Summary of the reading's core argument (1-2 sentences).
- Personal reaction / connection.
- Broader implication or question.
