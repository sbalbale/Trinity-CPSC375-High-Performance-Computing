Reason: Matrix B Gets Too Big for Cache

• Elements of A are accessed sequentially (good spatial locality)
• Computing a row of C requires accessing every element of B.
• Elements of B are accessed with a large stride (n elements
apart), which often exceeds the size of a cache line.
• For large n, every access to B results in a cache miss.
• Reordering loops or blocking (or tiling) improves performance.

Strassen’s Matrix Multiplication
• The standard way to multiply two 2-by-2 matrices
involves 8 multiplications and 4 additions.
• Strassen’s insight was that you could compute the
product using only 7 multiplications, at the cost of more
additions.
• Since multiplication is computationally more expensive
than addition in terms of complexity, this trade-off results
in a faster overall runtime for large matrices.
• This can be done by the Divide and Conquer Approach.
