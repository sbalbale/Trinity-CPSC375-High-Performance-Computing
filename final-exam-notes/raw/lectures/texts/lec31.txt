• Process i computes Ci = Ai* x Bi, where Ai* refers to the

held by the processor.
• Process i sends its current block of B to its neighbor
Process i+1 and receives a new block of B from Process i-1
(with Process p sending to Process 1 to form the ring).
• This process repeats p-1 times.
• When every process has seen every block of B, and the
local stripe Ci is complete.

Fox’s Algorithm
• Uses a p x p checkerboard decomposition.
• While Cannon’s algorithm relies on shifting both
matrices A and B, Fox’s algorithm uses a broadcast-andshift approach.
• It is often considered more intuitive than Cannon's
because it mimics the outer-product definition of matrix
multiplication more closely.

31

Fox’s Algorithm
• To compute a block Cij, you need blocks from the i-th
row of A and the j-th column of B.
• Fox's algorithm achieves this through two primary
communication patterns in each step:
– In each step k, one processor in each row broadcasts its
block of A to all other processors in that same row.
– After the multiplication, blocks of matrix B are shifted
upwards by one position circularly.

32

Step-by-Step Execution
• Each process Pij starts with its assigned blocks Aij and Bij.
• Iteration k from 0 to q-1 (where q = p):
– In each row i, the process at column j = (i + k) (p mod q) is
designated as the broadcaster.
– The broadcaster sends its local block of A to all other q-1
processes in its row. Let's call this received block Ai*
– Each processor Pij multiplies the received Ai* with its
currently held block of B:
Cij = Cij + (Ai* x Blocal)
– Every processor sends its current B block to the neighbor
directly above it (Pi-1, j) and receives a new B block from the
neighbor below (Pi+1,j)
33

Fox vs Cannon
• Don't have to deal with the complex initial skewing logic
required by Cannon.
• Depending on the hardware, broadcast might be highly
optimized by the network hardware, e.g., MPI_Bcast ,
making Fox’s algorithm faster than the manual shifts of
Cannon.
• Bur, requires an extra buffer to store the broadcasted A
block, whereas Cannon is strictly in-place regarding
communication buffers.
