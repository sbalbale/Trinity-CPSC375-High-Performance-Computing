# APEX Trekking Module

Read this file when Sean asks about hiking, backpacking, Appalachian Trail sections,
or multi-day trekking during the off-season (June–August, Weston MA base).

---

## Metric Shift: Watts → Calories/Hour + Pack Weight %

In trekking mode, output metrics shift entirely. Power watts are irrelevant.

| Standard Training Metric | Trekking Equivalent |
|---|---|
| Watts (power) | Calories/Hour (terrain + pack dependent) |
| TSS | Daily caloric deficit + elevation gain/loss |
| FTP | Sustainable pace at target heart rate on grade |
| Recovery Score | Sleep quality + soreness + foot/joint status |

**Daily caloric expenditure range:** 3,500–6,000 kcal/day depending on topography, pack weight, and pace.

**Pack weight target:** ≤25% of bodyweight for multi-day efficiency. Above 30% = joint stress begins compounding.

---

## Elevation Loss Rule (APEX Interference Logic)

> **This rule mirrors the Alpine Skiing override and is equally non-negotiable.**

If a trekking session logs **>500m of cumulative elevation loss** (descent-heavy day):
- Eccentric quad/glute loading is significant (sarcomere disruption, DOMS onset 24–48h post).
- **Reduce lower-body gym volume by 50% for the following 48 hours.**
- Flag: `⚠️ DESCENT LOAD DETECTED — lower-body gym volume halved for 48h.`
- Stack with ski flag if both occurred within the same 72h window: full lower-body rest (no lifting at all).

For elevation gain (ascent-only): concentric dominant — apply standard TSS fatigue calculation. No special override unless cumulative gain exceeds 2,000m in a single day.

| Food | kcal/100g | Primary Macro | Notes |

**Practical application:** 1 liter of olive oil = ~7,700 kcal at ~900g. Enough to supplement 2 dinners per day for a full week's caloric buffer.

## Microclimatic Gear Matrix

### Connecticut / Mid-Atlantic (AT Sections: CT, MA, NY, NJ, PA)
- **Temperature:** 70–90°F daytime, 55–65°F night (summer)
- **Humidity:** High — sweat evaporation impaired. Heat exhaustion risk elevated.
- **Gear mandates:**
  - Ultra-lightweight, breathable fabrics (no cotton, no merino in summer heat)
  - Robust insect netting (head net + treated clothing or permethrin spray)
  - Electrolyte supplements mandatory — sodium loss is high in humid heat
  - Hydration: 1L/hour on steep ascent in direct sun; minimum 3L/day

### White Mountains / Northern New England (NH, ME)
- **Temperature:** 40–70°F day; can drop to 35–45°F at elevation overnight even in June
- **Risk profile:** Snow above 4,000 ft possible through June. Sudden weather change. High winds.
- **Gear mandates (non-negotiable additions):**
  - Sleep system rated to **30°F** minimum (quilt or sleeping bag)
  - Insulated sleeping pad (R-value ≥4)
  - Impermeable rain shell (not just water-resistant — full waterproof membrane)
  - Wind layer / puffy insulation for above-treeline sections
  - Traction devices (microspikes) if traveling before July 1 above 4,500 ft
- **Pack weight impact:** These mandates add ~1.5–2 kg vs. mid-Atlantic kit. Adjust caloric planning accordingly (higher cold-environment caloric burn).

## Hydration in Trekking Mode

Replace **1.5L of water per kg of bodyweight lost** (same post-session rule as training).
On trail: this means weighing before and after long descent/ascent sections if possible.

In humid mid-Atlantic summer: assume 0.5–1.0 kg/hour fluid loss on sustained uphills.
In White Mountains with high wind: fluid loss is deceptively high even in cold — do not reduce intake.

Water sources: filter all surface water (Sawyer Squeeze or equivalent). Plan resupply at shelters or towns for sections longer than 2 days between reliable sources.

## Caloric Planning Formula

```
Daily caloric need (trekking) = BMR × 1.8 to 2.2 (depending on grade and pack weight)

For Sean (approx. 185 lbs / 84 kg, male, ~20yo):
  BMR ≈ 2,000 kcal
  Trekking multiplier: 1.9–2.3 for full days with 8–15 miles + elevation
  Daily target: 3,800–4,600 kcal

Pack 10% buffer above target — caloric deficit accumulates across multi-day trips.
```

## Recovery After Return from Multi-Day Trek

After any trek of 3+ days:
- Mandatory 48h reduced-intensity transition before resuming normal training.
- Lower-body lift check: if cumulative descent exceeded 1,000m total across the trip, treat as repeated ski override. No heavy squats or deadlifts for 72h post-return.
- RRS will likely read Orange (40–64) for 1–2 days post-trek. Honor it.
- Prioritize protein and sleep. First 24h nutrition: high protein (1.8–2.0g/kg), moderate carbs, high hydration.
