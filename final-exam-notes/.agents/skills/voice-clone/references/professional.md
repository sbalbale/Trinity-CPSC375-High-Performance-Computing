# Professional Writing Samples

These are curated excerpts from Sean Balbale's professional and semi-professional communications. Use them as stylistic anchors when generating emails, cover letters, networking messages, and similar outreach.

---

## Sample 1: Cold Outreach to Coaches (Transfer Application)

> Dear Coaches MacDermott and Clark,
>
> My name is Sean Balbale, and I am a sophomore at Northeastern University. I am applying to transfer to Trinity for spring 2025.
>
> In high school, I was a member of the Wayland-Weston crew team and served as Varsity Captain. I rowed in the Varsity Eight (six-seat) and double (both bow and stroke seats) during my senior year.
>
> I plan to transfer because I miss rowing and want to be part of a tighter-knit community than I have found at Northeastern.
>
> My current 2k time is 6:50 at 165 lbs. Unfortunately, I could not join the Northeastern team. Since then, I've kept up training. I work out daily. My workouts consist of a mix of erging and lifting. If accepted to Trinity, I would love to join the team.
>
> Academically, I have maintained a 3.60 / 4.00 GPA while double majoring in Computer Engineering and Computer Science.
>
> I have filled out your recruiting form using my high school information and would happily send you any of my college information. I will keep you in the loop in lieu of any changes/updates.
>
> If your schedule permits, I would love to meet with you in person or on Zoom.
>
> Best regards,
> Sean Balbale

### What to notice:
- **Opens with identity + purpose** in sentence one. No fluff.
- **Short, punchy declarative sentences.** "I plan to transfer because I miss rowing." No hedging.
- **Specific data up front:** 2k time, weight, GPA — scannable in 3 seconds.
- **Closes with a low-friction ask:** "If your schedule permits" — gives the recipient an easy out.

## Sample 2: Networking Cold Outreach — Trinity Alum (Palantir)

> Dear Mr. Marsden,
>
> My name is Sean Balbale, and I'm a current junior at Trinity College (Class of 2027) pursuing dual degrees in Electrical & Computer Engineering and Computer Science.
>
> I'm reaching out because I found your profile on LinkedIn and was excited to see a fellow Trinity alum at Palantir. As a fellow Bantam and varsity athlete (I'm on the Men's Rowing team), I was particularly interested to see how you've built a career in tech after your time at Trinity.
>
> I am currently in the midst of my Summer 2026 internship search, and Palantir's technical roles — like the Forward Deployed Software Engineer — are at the top of my list. My background includes a backend software internship at Bullhorn (Java/Spring Boot) and several embedded systems projects.
>
> I know your role is in Business Development, but I would be incredibly grateful for 15-20 minutes of your time. I'd love to ask a few questions about your experience at Palantir and any advice you might have for an engineer navigating the recruiting process.
>
> I've attached my resume for context. Thank you for your time and consideration.
>
> Go Bantams!
>
> Best regards,
> Sean Balbale

### What to notice:
- **"Hello [Name]," vs. "Dear [Name],"**: Sean uses "Dear" only for truly formal/first-contact situations. Recruiters and professors get "Hello."
- **Connection points are layered**: shared school AND shared athletic identity, not just one.
- **Acknowledges the mismatch** ("I know your role is in Business Development, but...") — disarms the objection before it's made.
- **"15-20 minutes"** — specific and low-friction. Not "pick your brain."
- **"Go Bantams!"** — warm school-spirit closing used specifically with Trinity alumni.

## Sample 3: Networking Cold Outreach — Trinity Alum (Shopify)

> Dear Margie,
>
> My name is Sean Balbale, and I'm a junior at Trinity College (Class of 2027) pursuing dual degrees in Electrical & Computer Engineering and Computer Science. I found your profile on LinkedIn and was happy to see a fellow Bantam in a field that I'm interested in.
>
> I am currently starting my search for a Summer 2026 software engineering internship, and Shopify is at the top of my list. My most recent experience was a backend internship at Bullhorn, where I focused on migrating enterprise Java and Spring Boot applications.
>
> I read on your profile that you're passionate about helping people tell their stories and navigate their career paths. As a student trying to find my own path in engineering, I would be very grateful for 15-20 minutes of your time to ask a few questions about your experience and any advice you might have for a student hoping to join a company like Shopify.
>
> I've attached my resume for context. I know you have a very busy schedule, so I appreciate any guidance you can offer.
>
> Thank you for your time and consideration. Go Bantams!
>
> Best regards,
> Sean Balbale

### What to notice:
- **References the recipient's stated values** ("I read on your profile that you're passionate about helping people...") — personalizes the ask.
- **Acknowledges their time** with "I know you have a very busy schedule" — lowers resistance without being self-deprecating.

## Sample 4: Post-Networking Thank You (After Shopify Call)

> Hi Margie,
>
> Thank you so much for taking the time to chat with me today. I really appreciate your feedback on my resume and your advice on navigating the search for a software engineering role.
>
> Your point about the 3 to 5-second read really clicked for me. I'm going to start working on a new, software-specific version of my resume — focusing on a cleaner, more modern format, adding a clear header, and making sure my Bullhorn experience isn't getting lost in the text. I also plan to look into Ashby and Toast, as they seem like great targets for the type of product culture I'm looking for.
>
> I'll take you up on your generous offer to look at the revised resume once I've made those changes. I'll send that over to you soon.
>
> Thanks again for your time.
>
> Best regards,
> Sean Balbale

### What to notice:
- **References specific advice from the conversation** (the "3 to 5-second read" point) — proves he was listening.
- **States concrete next actions** — not just "thanks, I'll think about it."
- **Explicitly takes up the offer** ("I'll take you up on your generous offer") — locks in the follow-on without being pushy.
- **No filler opener.** Dives straight into gratitude + specifics.

## Sample 5: Internship Offer Acceptance (Sonos)

> Hi Nicole,
>
> I hope you are having a great weekend!
>
> Please forgive the delayed response; I have been swamped studying for finals.
>
> I am thrilled to accept the offer to join Sonos as a Software Engineering Intern this summer!
>
> Please send over the formal written offer letter. I will keep an eye out for the email during my study breaks and will get it signed and returned to you as soon as I can.
>
> Best regards,
> Sean Balbale

### What to notice:
- **"I hope you are having a great weekend!"** — one of Sean's accepted warm openers (warm occasion-specific variant, not the banned "finds you well").
- **Owns the delay with a reason:** "Please forgive the delayed response; I have been swamped studying for finals." — semicolon between apology and cause.
- **Enthusiastic but controlled.** "I am thrilled to accept" — clear, declarative.
- **Specifies the next action** so both parties know what happens next.

> Hi Nicole,

> Just submitted the signed offer letter; super excited to make it official!

> Quick question now that I'm locked in: I know I'm joining the Cloud/Service team (which is awesome), but since I'm a dual ECE major, I also really love the low-level embedded side. Is there typically much collaboration between the Cloud and Firmware/Embedded teams? If there's ever a chance to work on projects that bridge that gap this summer, I'd love to get involved.
>
> Best,
> Sean Balbale

### What to notice:
- **"Just submitted the signed offer letter; super excited..."** — semicolon links two closely related short clauses in a casual email.
- **Parenthetical warmth:** "(which is awesome)" — keeps the tone genuine, not kiss-up.
- **"Bridge that gap"** — correct use of the signature phrase, connecting ECE and CS/Cloud disciplines.
- Uses "Quick question" to signal the ask is low-stakes.

## Sample 8: Graceful Rejection Response (Palantir)

> Hi Carter,
>
> I received the update regarding my application today.
>
> While I'm naturally disappointed not to be moving forward, I appreciate the time you and the team took to get to know me. I have a lot of respect for Palantir's engineering culture, and the process has only reinforced my desire to work there in the future.
>
> I will be joining Sonos this summer as a Software Engineering Intern to continue building my skills in cloud services and embedded systems. I plan to take the team's advice to heart and will definitely be reapplying next year.
>
> Thanks again for your help throughout the process.
>
> Best regards,
> Sean Balbale

### What to notice:
- **"While I'm naturally disappointed... I appreciate..."** — the Pivot applied to a rejection. Concedes the emotion, then pivots to professional grace.
- **States what he's doing instead** — projects forward momentum, not defeat.
- **"Only reinforced my desire to work there in the future"** — turns the rejection into a renewed commitment, not bitterness.
- **Keeps it brief.** No wallowing.

## Sample 9: Technical Issue Report to Administrative Staff (TrinCore SSO)

> Hi Charmaine,
>
> I hope you are doing well.
>
> I am writing to let you know that I am currently unable to access the new TrinCore system to log my TA hours. When I try to use the Single Sign-On (SSO) link provided, I get an error and am unable to log in.
>
> Is there a step I am missing to get access to the new Oracle system, or should I reach out to IT directly to resolve this? I want to ensure I can enter my hours.
>
> Best regards,
> Sean Balbale

### What to notice:
- **"I hope you are doing well."** — accepted warm opener for a professional relationship that has some history.
- **Problem is stated clearly and immediately:** "I am currently unable to access..." — no burying the issue.
- **Describes the exact failure:** "When I try to... I get an error" — gives the reader what they need to diagnose the problem.
- **Offers two resolution paths** so the recipient can easily route the answer.
- **"I want to ensure I can enter my hours."** — states the underlying goal, not just the symptom.

## Sample 10: Scheduling Flexibility (Rescheduling with a Professor)

> Hello Professor Starr,
>
> I had 12:30 in my calendar, but if 11:30 works better for you, I can do that. I am on campus tomorrow, but I have class from 10 am to 4:10, so I could do 9 am tomorrow as well.
>
> Best,
> Sean Balbale

### What to notice:
- **Acknowledges his own record** ("I had 12:30 in my calendar") before accommodating — doesn't just capitulate.
- **Offers alternatives proactively** rather than just saying "sure, whatever works."
- **Ultra-short** — appropriate for a scheduling micro-email in an ongoing thread.
- **"Hello Professor Starr,"** — "Hello" as opener even in a quick reply to a professor.

## Key Email Patterns (Summary)

### Opening Lines:
- **Default professional:** "Hello [Name],"
- **Warm ongoing relationship:** "Hi [Name],"
- **Fully formal (first contact, admissions):** "Dear [Title] [Name],"
- **Warm opener (optional, max once per thread):** "I hope you are doing well." / "I hope you are having a great weekend!"
- **NEVER:** "I hope this email finds you well"

### Follow-Up Formula:
"I wanted to quickly follow up to confirm that you received my email above. [Reiterate the key point or ask.]"

### Delay Apology Formula:
"Please forgive the delayed response; I have been swamped [brief reason]."

### Graceful Rejection Formula:
"While I'm naturally disappointed not to be moving forward, I appreciate [specific acknowledgment]. [Pivot: what I'm doing instead.] I plan to [future action]."

### Scheduling Flexibility Formula:
"I had [original time] in my calendar, but if [alternative] works better for you, I can do that. I'm also available [other alternatives]."

### Closing:
- First contact / formal: "Best regards, Sean Balbale" + full signature block
- Standard professional: "Best regards, Sean Balbale" + signature block
- Casual follow-up: "Best, Sean Balbale" or "Thanks, Sean Balbale"
- Very brief reply: "Best, Sean"
- Alumni outreach: add "Go Bantams!" before sign-off
- Common closing request: "Please let me know [next step or if you need anything]."
