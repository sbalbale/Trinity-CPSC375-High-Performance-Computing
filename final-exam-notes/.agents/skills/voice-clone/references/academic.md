# Academic Writing Samples

These are curated excerpts from Sean Balbale's academic essays. Use them as stylistic anchors when generating academic content. Pay attention to: sentence architecture, the "Pivot" structure, vocabulary register, and argumentative flow.

---

## Sample 1: Literature Review Opening (RHET 125 — "Embodied Harms and Inferred Data")

> The emergence of Extended Reality (XR), an umbrella term for Virtual and Augmented Reality (VR/AR) technologies, represents a massive shift in human-computer interaction. Unlike in previous eras, when data collection was technically optional and primarily a function of active user input — think clicks, text entries, and conscious selections — immersive technologies require the continuous collection of intimate user data to function correctly. This transition from choosing to tell the computer what we want to unwittingly showing the computer who we are creates ethical and technical challenges that existing legal frameworks are ill-equipped to address.

### What to notice:
- Opens with a **definitional anchor** (XR definition) before pivoting to the argument.
- Uses the **em-dash** to insert a parenthetical list without breaking flow ("— think clicks, text entries, and conscious selections —").
- The sentence "This transition from choosing to tell the computer what we want to unwittingly showing the computer who we are" is a **parallel construction** that reframes the technical shift as a philosophical one.
- High-register vocabulary: "ill-equipped," "intimate user data," "continuous collection."

## Sample 2: The "Pivot" Structure in Action

> This paper argues that the distinct nature of inferred data in XR renders existing privacy-as-secrecy frameworks structurally obsolete. It posits that cognitive liberty (a user's ability to maintain intellectual autonomy) in the metaverse can be best preserved through a rights-based framework, such as the IEEE's "Bodyright," which reconceptualizes biometrics as inalienable property rather than transactional data.

### What to notice:
- **Thesis is a compound assertion**: names the problem (obsolescence), names the solution (Bodyright), and defines the mechanism (reconceptualization).
- Uses **parenthetical definitions** inline — "(a user's ability to maintain intellectual autonomy)" — to keep the reader oriented without breaking into a footnote.
- Vocabulary: "structurally obsolete," "reconceptualizes," "inalienable property."

## Sample 3: The "Walled Garden" Paradox — Concession → Assertion

> However, this model presents a paradox. While it benefits privacy, it is also a powerful mechanism for market dominance. By restricting access to the device's cameras and sensors, Apple limits what third-party developers can build, while simultaneously exempting its own operating system from the same restrictions. Thus, creating a first-party loophole: Apple can use the sensitive behavioural and hardware data it denies competitors (to optimize its own advertising and services) without technically sharing it.

### What to notice:
- Classic **"While [Concession], [Strong Assertion]"** pivot: "While it benefits privacy, it is also a powerful mechanism for market dominance."
- Builds the argument in **escalating layers**: paradox → mechanism → loophole → implication.
- Uses **colon** to introduce the punchline: "Thus, creating a first-party loophole:"
- Parenthetical adds specificity without derailing: "(to optimize its own advertising and services)."

## Sample 4: Legal/Technical Precision — The "Inference Gap"

> This definition creates a massive legal loophole. It focuses on identification (proving who you are) rather than inference (determining what you think or feel). XR systems track eye-gaze patterns, head orientation, and hand movements, not necessarily to identify the user (who is already logged in), but to infer their intent, attention, and emotional state. A user's concentration level or emotional reaction to an ad is not a "biometric identifier" under BIPA, but it is "inferred data" derived from biological inputs.

### What to notice:
- **Parenthetical definitions** used for instant clarity: "(proving who you are)" vs. "(determining what you think or feel)."
- Uses **"not X, but Y"** construction to sharpen the distinction.
- Vocabulary: "vitiate," "loophole," "biometric identifier," "inferred data."
- Quotes around legal terms ("biometric identifier," "inferred data") to flag them as contested concepts.

## Sample 5: Systems-Level Analysis — Connecting Economics to Architecture

> The 37% cost increase found in the University of Chicago study represents a "signal loss" that directly threatens the advertising revenue stream. If advertisers cannot target users effectively, they spend less, and the platform's revenue declines. Therefore, there is a direct, quantifiable, and significant financial incentive against data minimization. For Meta, the shift to the metaverse is an opportunity to reclaim the "signal" lost to privacy regulations like the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

### What to notice:
- **Grounds the argument in data** ("37% cost increase") before interpreting it.
- Uses a **causal chain**: signal loss → less spending → revenue decline → incentive against privacy.
- The word "Therefore" is used to signal logical deduction, not rhetorical flourish.
- Treats economic incentives as **architectural forces** — this is a signature Sean move.

## Sample 6: Platform Essay Opening (RHET 125 — "The Price of the Endless Scroll")

> To its more than one billion users, the TikTok experience is one of seamless, infinite entertainment. The For You Page (FYP) delivers a personalized infinite stream of short-form video content that appears on your device as if by magic. On TikTok, trends emerge overnight, and communities form around every niche. The perception of TikTok as a simple, user-focused application, however, obscured its true nature as a complex industrial system. To see the platform, one must look beyond the screen to the hidden factories of human labor and energy consumption that sustain its global operation.

### What to notice:
- Opens with the **user-facing perception** before pivoting to the hidden reality.
- Uses "however" as a **hinge** — the sentence physically turns from illusion to truth.
- "Hidden factories of human labor and energy consumption" — concrete, physical language to describe abstract systems.
- The thesis is a **contrast**: immaterial image vs. material reality.

## Sample 7: Conclusion — The Reframe

> The transition to the metaverse is not merely a visual upgrade; it is a structural shift in the political economy of data. [...] Therefore, protecting cognitive liberty in the metaverse requires transforming legal frameworks. We must move from the "notice-and-choice" model to a rights-based "Bodyright" framework. This framework must treat biometric and behavioral data not as raw material for extraction, but as an extension of the human self—inalienable, protected, and sovereign.

### What to notice:
- **"Not X; it is Y"** reframe in the opening line.
- Uses **"We must"** — active, first-person-plural call to action.
- The final em-dash ("the human self—inalienable, protected, and sovereign") lands a **tricolon** (three-part list) for rhetorical force.
- Does NOT use "In conclusion" — uses the reframe itself as the signal that we're concluding.
